01
What InsightIQ does
InsightIQ is a procurement decision support system. It watches an inventory ledger,
notices when a product is running out, works out who should supply the replacement
stock, checks that supplier against a set of business rules, and produces a costed
purchase order with a delivery date — then explains, step by step, why it made every
one of those choices.
The work is split across four specialist agents arranged in a pipeline, coordinated by a
fifth. Each agent has one job and hands a structured result to the next. Nothing is a
black box: the Knowledge Agent emits a full reasoning trace that the interface replays
as an animation, so a human reviewer can follow the logic rule by rule before approving
anything.
THE CORE IDEA
Ranking and risk are deliberately kept apart. The Supplier Intelligence Agent answers “who scores best?”
using a weighted heuristic. The Knowledge Agent independently answers “does this supplier break any
rules?” A high score can never override a risk rule — that separation is what makes the recommendation
defensible.

Bridging two disconnected supplier worlds
The two supplier datasets do not share a join key, and each is missing what the other
has. The logistics feed tracks ten numeric suppliers ( SUP_1 … SUP_10 ) with live cost,
stock and lead-time data but no quality signal. The procurement KPI file tracks five
named vendors with rich quality history but no live pricing or stock.
Ingestion reconciles them by deterministically round-robin assigning each live supplier
a named vendor as its quality benchmark, producing one pool that carries both. Three
fields are synthesised in the process:
— Quality rating, from the KPI vendor's history (clipped to 1–5)
Rating = 5 × (0.4·FulfillmentRate + 0.3·ComplianceRate + 0.3·(1 − Def
— Reliability, from how *consistent* the supplier's lead time is
(low variance = dependable). Min-Max inverted, then clipped to 0.5–
Reliability = 1 − MinMax(LeadTimeStdDev)
— Delivery estimate, blending live telemetry with historical KPI reco
DeliveryTime = 0.7 × LiveAvgLeadTime + 0.3 × HistoricalDeliveryDays
WORTH KNOWING
Because reliability is clipped to a 0.5–1.0 floor, four of the ten suppliers sit at exactly 0.500 . They are
indistinguishable on that axis, and only one supplier ( SUP_4 , at 1.000) clears the Knowledge Agent's
0.90 reliability bar. That is a property of how the source data is generated, not a scoring fault.

04
Perception Agent
The Perception Agent observes the environment and emits structured facts. It answers
one question: which products need replenishing, and by how much? It makes no
decisions — it only reports state.
FORMULAS
TargetStock = ReorderLevel × 3 — cover lead-time demand plus s
Deficit = max(0, TargetStock − UnitsInStock)
Urgent = UnitsInStock < ReorderLevel
The multiplier of 3 encodes the policy that healthy stock is three times the reorder
point. Note the two thresholds do different jobs: Urgent decides whether to act, while
Deficit decides how much to buy — and it is measured against the target, not the
reorder point, so an order restores a comfortable buffer rather than scraping the
minimum.
WORKED EXAMPLE — SKU-0717 “HEADPHONES”
Inventory = 144 units
ReorderPoint = 928 units
TargetStock = 928 × 3 = 2,784
Deficit = 2,784 − 144 = 2,640 units
Urgent = 144 < 928 = TRUE → flagged for procurement
Alongside the focus SKU, the agent scans the full ledger (currently 53 urgent SKUs) and
aggregates demand signals — top buyers, demand by country, and a twelve-month
order trend — which feed the dashboard.
05
Supplier Intelligence Agent
This agent performs the heuristic search. It takes the deficit from Perception, narrows
the supplier pool to those that can actually fill it, scores each one on four weighted
attributes, and returns a ranked candidate list. This is the mathematical core of the
system, so it is worth taking slowly.

=== PAGE 5 ===
Step 1 — Eligibility filtering
A supplier that cannot cover the order is not scored at all. Filtering happens before any
normalisation, which matters for the step that follows.
IF stock_availability ≥ Deficit → eligible
ELSE → excluded
If no supplier can single-handedly cover the deficit, the agent falls back to ranking the
entire pool rather than failing, so a partial or split-order strategy remains possible
downstream.
Step 2 — Min–Max normalisation over the eligible pool only
The four attributes are measured in incompatible units — rupees, days, stars, a ratio.
They are made comparable with Min–Max normalisation, which rescales any value to a
0–1 range:
N(x) = (x − Min(x)) / (Max(x) − Min(x))
Min and Max are taken from the eligible pool, not the full pool. A supplier that was
filtered out cannot stretch the scale — scores always express “how good is this supplier
compared with the ones actually in contention.”
Step 3 — Direction correction
Raw normalisation always maps the minimum to 0 and the maximum to 1, but the
minimum isn't always the best. Cheap is good; slow is bad. Before weighting, every
component is flipped where necessary so that all four point the same way:
COMPONENT DIRECTION FORMULA SCORE OF 0 SCORE OF 1
Rating Higher is better Rating / 5 Lowest rated Highest rated
Cost Lower is better 1 − N(Cost) Most expensive Cheapest
Reliability Higher is better N(Reliability) Least reliable Most reliable
Delivery Lower is better 1 − N(DeliveryTime) Slowest Fastest
Rating is the exception that is not Min–Max normalised. It already occupies a fixed,
externally meaningful 0–5 scale, so it is simply divided by 5. Normalising it would make
a 3-star supplier score 0 purely because it happened to be the worst in a strong field.
InsightIQ — Technical Reference 5

=== PAGE 6 ===
Step 4 — The weighted Raw Score
The four direction-corrected components are combined with fixed business weights.
Cost and Reliability together account for exactly half the score:
RawScore = 0.30 × RatingScore
+ 0.25 × CostScore
+ 0.25 × ReliabilityScore
+ 0.20 × DeliveryScore
Fully expanded:
RawScore = 0.30 × (Rating / 5)
+ 0.25 × (1 − (Cost − MinCost) / (MaxCost − MinCost))
+ 0.25 × ((Rel − MinRel) / (MaxRel − MinRel))
+ 0.20 × (1 − (Dlv − MinDlv) / (MaxDlv − MinDlv))
Weights total 1.00, so RawScore is bounded 0 … 1
PRIORITY WEIGHT REASONING
Rating 30% Overall vendor quality carries the most weight
Cost 25%
Together exactly 50% — commercial terms and dependability are the deciding
pair
Reliability 25%
Delivery 20% Speed matters, but least of the four
Step 5 — The Final Score (0–100)
Raw Scores are rescaled once more, to a presentation-friendly 0–100 band:
FinalScore = ((RawScore − MinRawScore) / (MaxRawScore − MinRawScore))

=== PAGE 7 ===
READ THIS CORRECTLY
Final Score is a relative ranking score, nothing more. Because it is rescaled across the pool, the best
supplier always scores exactly 100 and the worst exactly 0 — regardless of how good either actually is. It
is not a safety score, not a risk probability, and not a percentage chance of success. Risk is the
Knowledge Agent's job.

06
Knowledge Agent
The Knowledge Agent applies forward chaining — the classic data-driven inference
strategy from expert systems. It starts from known facts, finds every rule whose
conditions those facts satisfy, fires them to derive new facts, then sweeps the rule set
again. It repeats until a pass produces nothing new: a fixed point.
InsightIQ — Technical Reference 8

=== PAGE 9 ===
working memory ← facts from Perception + Supplier Intelligence
REPEAT
FOR each rule not yet fired:
IF its conditions hold against working memory:
fire it → assert the new fact
UNTIL no rule fires in a full pass (fixed point reached)
Every firing — matched or not — is appended to a structured reasoning trace, which is
what makes the decision auditable and what the XAI inspector animates.
The rule set
RULE 1 — INVENTORY STATE
IF Inventory < ReorderPoint
THEN State = "Urgent Procurement"
ELSE State = "Monitoring"
RULE 2 — PREFERRED SUPPLIER
IF Rating ≥ 4.5 AND DeliveryTime ≤ 3 days
THEN Supplier_Status = "Preferred"
Both conditions must hold. This is a demanding bar, and no supplier in the current pool
meets it — the best rating is 4.39 and the fastest delivery is 7.9 days.
RULE 3 — RISK ASSESSMENT
IF DefectRate > 0.05 (5%)
THEN Supplier_Status = "High Risk"
A strict inequality, which makes the boundary sharp: a defect rate of exactly 0.05 does
not trigger the rule, while 0.0502 does. This precision is preserved end to end — the
agent receives unrounded values specifically so display formatting can never flip a
verdict.
InsightIQ — Technical Reference 9

=== PAGE 10 ===
RULE 4 — RELIABILITY WITH FALLBACK
IF no prior status AND Reliability ≥ 0.90
THEN Supplier_Status = "Reliable"
ELSE Supplier_Status = "Standard"
Rule 4 only evaluates if Rules 2 and 3 both left the supplier unlabelled — the no prior
status guard. It is the safety net that guarantees every supplier ends up with a status,
defaulting to Standard.
Status precedence
A supplier can accumulate more than one label in a sweep. Precedence resolves it, and
High Risk always wins:
High Risk ▸ Preferred ▸ Reliable ▸ Standard

07
Selecting the recommended supplier
This is where ranking and rules meet. The rule is deliberately simple:
Walk the heuristic ranking from rank 1 downward.
Recommend the first candidate whose status is not "High Risk".
The heuristic proposes; the rule engine vetoes. A supplier can top the ranking and still
be rejected, and no score is high enough to overrule a risk flag.

The backup supplier is chosen the same way: the highest-ranked remaining candidate

SUP_5 . Designating a rejected supplier as the contingency would contradict the very

A NOTE ON THIS DATASET
The eligibility filter never actually excludes anyone here: the largest deficit across all 53 urgent SKUs is
2,723 units, while the smallest supplier stock is 6,670. Every supplier clears every SKU with room to
spare, so the ranking is identical for every product. The filtering mechanism is correct and demonstrably
active at higher deficits — this dataset simply never exercises it.
08
Planning Agent
The final agent converts a decision into an executable plan: how much to order, when it
will arrive, what it will cost, and who to fall back on.
ORDER QUANTITY
OrderQuantity = roundUpToNearest10( max(Deficit, 10) )
Rounded up to a practical order multiple, with a floor so a trivial deficit still produces a
viable order.
ARRIVAL DATE
Lead time is counted in working days — weekends are skipped, not merely subtracted
— then a seasonality buffer is added. That buffer is derived from the calendar table
rather than hard-coded: seasons with a higher density of non-working days are treated
as higher-friction shipping periods and earn 0–2 extra working days.
baseLeadDays = round(supplier.delivery_time_days)
naiveArrival = addWorkingDays(today, baseLeadDays) Mon–Fri only
finalArrival = addWorkingDays(naiveArrival, seasonBuffer) 0–2 days
